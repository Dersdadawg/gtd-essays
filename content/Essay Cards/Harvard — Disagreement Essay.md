## Prompt

> [!question] Prompt
> Describe a time when you strongly disagreed with someone about an idea or issue. How did you communicate or engage with this person? What did you learn from this experience?

- **What works:** The opening question hooks the reader. The ending, where your friend "had been walking away from Omelas the whole time," is elegant.
- **Problem: you come off badly in the argument.** You "performed the felicific calculus as outrageously as I could" and "continued to push him." The prompt asks how you engaged with the other person, and the answer here is "I trolled him until he said something I couldn't refute."
- **Problem: the lesson reads as giving up.** "Some things are just wrong" is an assertion, not an argument. Saying you "couldn't subvert or refute" it can sound like you converted because you ran out of arguments. A conversion story is fine, but show one moment of real thinking on your part.
- **Problem: the "extra mile" section is abstract.** It never shows what you now do differently in a disagreement. One concrete example of the new behavior would do more than the two lines about miles.
- **Mechanics:** "I continued to push him, when his mantra came" is awkward; rewrite it. "Bentham's bastards" is a small risk that some readers will dislike.
